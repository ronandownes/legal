Family Occupation & Subsequent Letting

Where a tenancy is ended on the basis that a landlord or family member intends to occupy the dwelling, the stated intention should be assessed against what actually happens afterwards.

An advertisement for unrelated occupants may be relevant evidence. Actual occupation by unrelated paying occupants can be stronger evidence. There is no single number of unrelated occupants that automatically proves that the original family-occupation ground was false.

The evidence should be considered cumulatively: the statutory declaration, the identity of the proposed family occupier, the expected duration of occupation, the timing of any advertisements, who actually occupies the property, rental arrangements, relevant statements and whether the stated family occupation genuinely occurs.

A property does not necessarily have to become fully occupied before concerns can become evidentially significant. Equally, the presence of an unrelated occupant does not by itself establish that a family-occupation ground was false. The legal position depends on the full chronology and the applicable tenancy law.

Evidence principle: observe and preserve what independently occurs. Keep dated screenshots, advertisements, URLs, photographs and contemporaneous notes. Do not provoke, induce or interfere with another person’s conduct in order to manufacture evidence.
